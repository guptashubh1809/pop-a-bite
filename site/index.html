FROM DOGRA KITCHENS

Sund. Jammu's own winter sweet.

A Dogra family recipe of almonds, cashews, dates, coconut and gond, roasted slowly in desi ghee. In Jammu, it turns up at life's biggest moments.

  • Before a baby arrives

    In Dogra families, a mother-to-be's own family sends her Sund in the last month before her baby is born.

  • At Lohri

    When the cold sets in and families gather around the Lohri fire, Sund is part of the celebration.

  • At weddings

    From one family to another, Sund has its place at Dogra weddings too.

FROM OUR FAMILIES

What people say after the first bite.

Why we started.

In most Indian homes, ladoos were never bought. Maa made them. She roasted the flour in ghee, melted the jaggery and rolled each one by hand, with a jar kept aside for the kids and another for the grandparents.

Life got busier. Fewer homes have the hours it takes, and what's on the shelf is often made with palm oil, refined sugar and flavourings.

So our Maa makes them for you, in our home kitchen in Jammu, the way she always has. She buys ingredients in small batches, never in bulk, starts cooking when your order comes in, and rolls every ladoo by hand.

That's all Pop-A-Bite is: the ladoos you grew up with, for every home that misses them.

GOOD TO KNOW

A few things before your first bite.

Is it really made at home?

Yes. Every batch is made by Maa in our home kitchen in Jammu, and every ladoo is rolled by hand. Pop-A-Bite is registered with FSSAI (Reg. No. 21026153000396).

Why does my order take a few days?

Because we start only when you order. Maa gets in whatever ingredients the order needs, then roasts and rolls everything by hand. How long it takes depends on how much you order and how many orders are ahead of yours. We'll tell you the exact date on WhatsApp before you pay.

How do you buy your ingredients?

We buy ghee, jaggery and dry fruits in small quantities and never stock up in bulk, so nothing is kept long enough to go stale. For a small order, Maa may use dry fruits from a recent batch; for bigger orders she buys new ones.

What is Sund?

Sund is a Dogra speciality from Jammu: almonds, cashews, dates, coconut and gond roasted slowly in desi ghee. Families make it in winter and for Lohri, serve it at weddings, and send it to a daughter in the last month before her baby is born. Eat a spoonful as it is, or with a glass of warm milk.

What is sattu?

Sattu is roasted chana (Bengal gram) ground into flour. It has been an everyday food in Bihar for generations, stirred into drinks, stuffed into parathas and rolled into ladoos, because it's filling and needs no more cooking. Our Sattu Ladoo has 11.6 g protein per 100 g, tested in a lab.

Why put haldi in a ladoo?

Our Haldi Ladoo comes from the family of North Indian winter ladoos, like pinni and gond ladoo: whole wheat and gond roasted in ghee, sweetened with jaggery. Maa adds haldi, the same spice that goes into haldi doodh on cold nights. It gives the ladoo its golden colour and a gentle, earthy warmth.

Why jaggery and not sugar?

It's what ladoos were always made with at home. Jaggery is sugarcane juice boiled down, without the refining that white sugar goes through, and it gives a deeper, caramel taste. We never use refined sugar or corn syrup.

Why ghee and not oil?

Ghee is how ladoos have always been roasted and bound. We use desi cow ghee only, and never palm oil, vanaspati or refined oils.

Up to 3 months. Store them at room temperature, away from direct sunlight.
